What "recall" and "precision" mean for a portfolio

Recall is the share of eventual bankruptcies the model actually caught in advance. Precision is how often a "distress" flag turns out to be right.

Old approach

Altman Z-Score

Catches 69% of failures — but for every 15 firms it flags, only 1 actually goes bankrupt. Too noisy to act on directly.

New approach

XGBoost

Catches 58% of failures, and when it flags a firm, it's right 80% of the time — a far more actionable signal for screening or diligence.

How the model was built

Dataset: UCI's Polish Companies Bankruptcy data (2000–2013), 65 financial ratios per firm-year. Missing values filled with the median; the training set was rebalanced with SMOTE since bankruptcies are rare (4.8%) — the 8,681-firm test set was left untouched at its real-world rate. Random Forest, XGBoost and a Neural Network were each tuned via grid search optimizing for recall, then compared against the classic Altman Z'-Score.

Not investment advice. This is a research and educational model built on historical Polish company data from 2000–2013. Financial ratios that predicted distress in that market and period may not transfer directly to other geographies, sectors, or time horizons. Use as one input among many, alongside qualitative diligence.
